# Interview - Elena Vasquez, BSA Compliance Officer (SYNTHETIC)

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a standard onboarding from the compliance chair.

**Elena:** From my chair it's four questions. Do we know who they are? Do we know who owns them, if it's an entity? Do we understand what the relationship is for? And could I defend the file to an examiner without being in the room to narrate it? The steps - identification, verification, screening, risk rating, beneficial ownership certification, EDD where triggered - exist to answer those questions. When the file answers them, I don't much care who typed what in which order.

**Interviewer:** What does the manual not tell you?

**Elena:** Two things everyone in this building knows and no document says. First, the ownership threshold. The certification rule is 25 percent - that's the law and we follow it. But operationally, for entities out of high-risk jurisdictions, my analysts start pulling threads at 20 percent. An owner at 22 percent of a Zubaria-incorporated entity is getting looked at like a 30 percent owner anywhere else. I know it happens, I think it's the right instinct, and I'm also aware that an undocumented internal threshold is exactly the kind of thing an examiner asks about at the worst moment. It's on my list to codify. It's been on my list for a while.

**Interviewer:** When do you escalate, and why?

**Elena:** The interesting version of that question is how things escalate to me. The formal path is an EDD ticket. The actual path, for the delicate ones - politically exposed persons, close associates especially - is that a senior analyst walks over and tells me verbally before any ticket exists. We talk it through, then the ticket appears, already shaped by that chat. I won't pretend I hate it; the conversations are genuinely useful. But there's no trail for the first half of the decision, and if I'm hit by a bus, that institutional judgment evaporates. I've asked twice for the ticket to come first. The walk-overs continue.

**Interviewer:** Describe a case that went wrong.

**Elena:** An applicant with only a foreign tax ID - no domestic identifiers at all. Perfectly legitimate fact pattern, and we had no written procedure for it. Still don't. The rep improvised, the file was thin, and it aged forty days while people played hot potato. What actually happens now is those cases go ad hoc to our EDD specialist, who does a genuinely good review - but "ask Fatima" is not a procedure. That's a gap, and I own that it's still open.

**Interviewer:** Which rules do you apply more strictly than written?

**Elena:** I make people write things down. The rule book doesn't require half the memos I demand. Paper is cheap; reconstructing a decision two years later in front of an examiner is not.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you'd change?

**Elena:** Budget for a second EDD specialist, and a board reporting template that doesn't require me to re-derive the same metrics quarterly. Neither is a process divergence - that's just the job asking for more hours than exist.
