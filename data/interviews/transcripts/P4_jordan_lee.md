# Interview — Jordan Lee, Frontline Onboarding Rep (SYNTHETIC)

*Synthetic transcript. Persona P4, tenure 2 years. Generated per data/interviews/SYNTHETIC.md.*

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a standard onboarding, start to finish.

**Jordan:** Customer applies, I collect the basics — name, DOB, address, ID number — and upload their documents. System kicks off screening, I do the document checks, jurisdiction gets scored, entities get the ownership certification, then risk rating and the decision, or it goes up to the analysts if anything's weird. On a clean individual it's twenty minutes. The portal eating uploads on the first try adds ten more, but that's a software complaint, not a process one.

**Interviewer:** What does the manual not tell you?

**Jordan:** What to do with real customers. Example: guy comes in, passport expired three weeks ago, and he's holding the official renewal receipt from the passport office. Manual says unexpired ID, period — it just doesn't contemplate the receipt situation. What we actually do: accept the expired passport plus the renewal receipt, open the account, and set a 30-day follow-up task to collect the new passport. Everyone on the floor does it. Tom's team runs the follow-up queue. Works great, exists nowhere in writing. I asked in training why it isn't in the manual and got a shrug.

**Interviewer:** When do you escalate, and why?

**Jordan:** The unwritten one everybody learns in week two: if two documents disagree on where the customer lives — two address mismatches, like the license says one thing and the bank statement says another — that goes straight to EDD referral, automatically. No policy page says it. First time I asked my mentor where that rule was written, she laughed. But you do it, because the one time you don't, it turns out to matter.

**Interviewer:** Which written steps get skipped in practice, and why?

**Jordan:** Honestly? The phone callback. For small accounts — under $10k expected activity — we skip the callback most of the time. You call, they don't pick up, you call again, voicemail, meanwhile the account's just sitting there and the customer's asking why. For a $3k checking account the callback catches nothing we didn't already know from the documents. I know QA hates it. I'd rather they just changed the rule to match what the risk actually is instead of us all quietly doing the same thing.

**Interviewer:** Describe a case that went wrong.

**Jordan:** A PO box one. Applicant's mailing address was a PO box — she lived rurally and that's just how her mail works. I accepted it with a supplemental document tying her to the physical location, which to me is exactly what policy silence plus common sense adds up to. QA — Marcus — bounced it and made her produce a utility bill for the physical address, three extra days, customer nearly walked. Same file, two different rules depending on who reads it. Pick one! I'd honestly accept either.

**Interviewer:** Which rules do you apply more strictly than written?

**Jordan:** Expected activity. The form lets people write anything; I push back on numbers that don't match the story — nobody's landscaping business does $2M cash a month. That's not in the manual either, but nobody's complained yet.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**Jordan:** Let customers upload documents from their phone without the portal timing out, and half my problems disappear. The other half are the callback rule and the PO box thing, and those aren't mine to fix.
