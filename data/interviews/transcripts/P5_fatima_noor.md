# Interview - Fatima Noor, EDD Specialist (SYNTHETIC)

*Synthetic transcript. Persona P5, tenure 7 years. Generated per data/interviews/SYNTHETIC.md.*

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a standard onboarding - from the EDD side.

**Fatima:** By the time a case reaches me it's already interesting. I get referrals: risk rating over threshold, list hits that survived first review, complex ownership, plus the informal channels we'll apparently be discussing. I build the enhanced file - source of funds, purpose of relationship, adverse media, ownership tracing - and either clear it with conditions, or recommend decline, or park it with monitoring. My rule is that the file should tell the story without me in the room.

**Interviewer:** What are the referral triggers, officially and actually?

**Fatima:** Officially: risk score, list hits, high-risk jurisdictions, PEP status. Actually, also: the address rule. If a case shows two address mismatches across documents, it comes to me automatically - the reps treat that as an iron law. It's written nowhere. It's also, for what it's worth, a genuinely good trigger; mismatched addresses correlate with half my real findings. Somebody should write it down before it stops being true and nobody notices.

**Interviewer:** When do things escalate to you informally?

**Fatima:** The hallway tickets. PEP close-associate cases in particular - a senior analyst will walk over and brief Elena verbally before any ticket exists, they'll settle the shape of it, and then a ticket materializes on my queue pre-marinated. I get why: those cases are delicate and the chat is faster than the form. But I open the ticket and the first hour of thinking already happened off the record. I've started writing "per prior verbal discussion" in my files just so future-me knows the trail starts before the timestamp.

**Interviewer:** Describe a case that went wrong.

**Fatima:** Screening missed a listed individual because his name was transliterated - the tool scored the variant spellings below the match threshold. The seniors now widen the match tolerance manually whenever a name looks transliterated, and re-run. On one hand: good catch discipline. On the other: match tolerance as a per-analyst judgment call is how you get two different screening outcomes for the same name, and I cannot put "Priya loosens the knob" in an exam response. The matcher needs transliteration support natively. I've written that memo twice.

**Interviewer:** Any category with no procedure at all?

**Fatima:** Foreign tax ID only - applicant has no domestic identifiers, just a foreign tax number. There's no written path for it, so those land on my desk ad hoc, by custom rather than by rule. I do a full enhanced review each time because with no procedure, over-doing it is the only defensible position. It's my most reliable source of unpaid overtime.

**Interviewer:** Which rules do you apply more strictly than written?

**Fatima:** Source-of-funds documentation. Policy asks for it in EDD; I require it to actually add up - a spreadsheet that reconciles. Paper that doesn't reconcile is decoration.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**Fatima:** A case-linking tool. Related-party cases live in five tabs and my memory, and my memory is not a control. That's a tooling wish, though - the process itself, where it's written at all, is mostly sane.
